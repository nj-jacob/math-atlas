# How Math Atlas is built

Math Atlas is a set of plain web pages with no build step: what is in this repository is exactly what the browser loads. It is hosted on Firebase at https://ontario-math-atlas.web.app.

## Folders

| Folder or file | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html`, `curriculum.html` | Atlas home page (Math plan and Language side by side; for parents and tutors, one card per child with both subjects) and the Curriculum page (Math and Language, every grade and chapter). Both share the top bar and sign-in from `assets/home.js` and `home.css`. `assets/curriculum.js` runs the Math / Language switch and builds the Language list. |
| `language/` (not in this repository) | The Language pages, from the `language-atlas` repository. The publish workflow copies them in before publishing (see "One app, two repositories" below and `SETUP.md`). |
| `family.html`, `live.html` | Family sign-in and parent and tutor pages, and the "Work together" page |
| `404.html` | The "page not found" page. Firebase shows it for any address that does not exist. It uses `/` paths so it works at any depth. |
| `plan/` | Math Plan: placement test and learning plans, Grades 1 to 8 |
| `grade1/` … `grade8/` | One site per grade (see below) |
| `assets/engine/` | **Shared by every grade**: the grade app (`site.js`), lesson engine, toolbox, answer types |
| `assets/atlas-theme.css` | The one look for every page |
| `assets/atlas-header.js` | The shared header on grade sites ("All grades" link to the Curriculum page) |
| `assets/icon.svg` | The site icon shown in browser tabs and bookmarks. Every page links to it. |
| `assets/load-note.js` | On the family and "Work together" pages: if sign-in cannot load, shows a short note instead of a blank page |
| `assets/fb.js`, `sync.js`, `family.js`, `live.js`, `langplace.js` | Sign-in and saving progress (Firebase); `langplace.js` shows Language placement results on the parent pages |
| `assets/review.js` | Review notes: the grown-up "Note" button on grade pages (see "Review notes" below) |
| `tests/` | Automatic checks (see `CHECKS.md`) |
| `.github/workflows/` | `checks.yml` runs the checks on pull requests. `deploy.yml` runs them again, then publishes `main`. |

## One grade site

`gradeN/index.html` loads, in this order:
1. `../assets/engine/site.js`: the grade app, the same for every grade (router, home page, practice, quiz, check-up, progress, reading settings). It creates the `Atlas` object.
2. `gradeN/assets/grade.js`: that grade's settings, in one `Atlas.setup({ ... })` call, and the grade's own drawing helpers for its chapters (see "Grade settings" below).
3. `../assets/engine/qkit.js`: the answer types (`Atlas.Q`).
4. `../assets/engine/manip.js`: the toolbox of manipulatives (`Manip`: base-ten blocks, number lines, coins and more). It also draws the Toolbox page (`Manip.toolbox`): one tool fills the screen under a slim bar (back, Undo, Show number, Tools), and the Tools menu groups the grade's tools on five shelves (Numbers; Parts and groups; Patterns and equations; Shapes and space; Measure, money and data). A tool's shelf, name, icon and settings are in the `BOX` list there. The Toolbox version of the base-ten blocks (`drag: true`) is dragged from a tray, has a Thousands column, and keeps the mat one size for the screen: as blocks are added they shrink to fit instead of the mat growing. Its number (when "Show number" is on) sits above the mat. Lessons use the same blocks with buttons. The Toolbox number line (`Manip.numberline` with `tool: true`) shares that frame: markers from a tray, jumps drawn with a handle or the Jump by buttons, zoom by tapping the line, Nearest ten and Nearest hundred, and a line that stands up on a phone. Lessons keep the simpler number line. The Toolbox counters (`Manip.counters` with `tool: true`, named "Counters and ten-frames") replace the old separate Counters and Ten-frames tools: red and yellow counters from a tray, a free mat or 1 or 2 ten-frames, tap a counter to turn it over, and Pair up, Rows of ten and Share into 2 to 10 groups. Up to 100 counters. Lessons keep the simpler counters and ten-frames. The Toolbox number chart (`Manip.hundred` with `tool: true`, named "Number chart") shows 1 to 100, 101 to 200 or 0 to 99: colour squares with 3 pens, skip count by 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 or 25, tap two numbers to see a jump as tens down and ones across, and hide some numbers to find. Lessons keep the simpler hundred chart. The Toolbox fraction kit (`Manip.fractions` with `tool: true`, named "Fraction kit") holds one amount made of pieces (1 whole, 1/2 to 1/10, up to 3 wholes), tapped or dragged from a tray. "See it as" shows the same amount at once as strips under the whole, circles, a set of counters and a point on a number line. "Do" has Share (things shared by 2 to 10 people, the leftovers cut, one share put on the mat), Split (cut every piece into 2 to 5), Line up (up to 3 amounts to compare), Fill wholes (pack pieces into whole bars or break a whole into pieces) and Count on. Grades 1 and 2 read the amount in words (3 fourths), Grade 3 on in numbers (3/4). Grade 4 and up (stage 2) adds decimal pieces (0.1; 0.01 from Grade 5; 0.001 from Grade 6), a Grid window (10 by 10, with a hundredth zoomed into thousandths from Grade 6), a Symbols window (fraction, simplest form, tenths or hundredths, mixed number, decimal and, from Grade 5, percent), Split into 10, Count on to 10 and Groups of. Grade 5 adds twelfths, Join (simplify), Combine and take away, and Fit into (whole number divided by a fraction). Grade 6 adds Cut into same-size pieces (unlike denominators) and typed fractions, decimals or percents in Line up. To keep the screen calm, from Grade 4 the tray has Fractions and Decimals tabs, and from Grade 5 the Do row shows one set at a time: Explore (Share, Split, Join, Line up, Fill wholes, Count on) or Calculate (Combine, Groups of, Fit into). Pieces too thin to see clearly (hundredths, thousandths) open the Grid. Fractions are written stacked (numerator over denominator) everywhere in the kit except number line labels; a hidden slash keeps them readable as 3/4 to screen readers and the checks, and on a very full mat the piece labels go back to 3/4 so they fit. Grade 7 and up (stage 3) adds **Times** (in Calculate) with an **Area** window: the first amount as columns of a square, the fraction taken as rows, and the overlap as the answer (3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12 = 1/2); in Grade 8 the first amount can be a mixed number (up to 3 wholes). The strips are off in Times, so First amount and Times buttons pick the row the tray fills. **Fit into** can fill an amount instead of wholes (3/4 ÷ 2/3 = 1 1/8), with Cut into same-size pieces; in Grade 8 the piece can be up to 3 wholes. **Line up** gets Plus and Minus (negative fractions: strips go left from 0 in the middle, and the number line runs below 0) and **Find one between** (cuts two amounts into the same pieces, finer each time, and adds one between them: 1/3 < 5/12 < 1/2; tenths become hundredths for decimals). Grade 8 can type a number on the plain mat to see percents over 100% or under 1% (150% = 1 1/2, 0.5% = 1/200). The mat leaves room at the bottom for however many rows of buttons there are. Grade 3 and up (stage 4) adds **Ratio** (in Calculate from Grade 5): the tray sets two amounts and what they are (beads, a juice mix, apples for children, and from Grade 6 prices), and the Ratio windows (two-letter keys starting with r, shown only in Ratio) draw them as counters in groups, a bar of units, a double number line, a table, a graph (Grade 7 and up) and symbols. The Times buttons scale it up (1 to 5 = 3 to 15). Grade 3 keeps the first amount at 1 and picks the second from 2, 5 and 10. Grade 6 adds Part of the whole (3 : 2 juice is 60% of the mix) and For 1 (4 for $6 = 1 for $1.50); Grade 7 adds Add beside Times, to show that adding the same to both changes the mix (off the graph's line); Grade 8 adds Compare two mixes (as percents) and Find the missing number (a typed proportion like 3 : 5 = ? : 20). Each of these turns on the windows that show it best. Lessons keep the simpler fraction strips. The Toolbox **Balance** (`Manip.balance`, on the Patterns and equations shelf, every grade) is a pan balance: cubes (1, in two colours), tens (Grade 2 and up), bags holding a hidden number x (Grade 4 and up), 0.1 pieces (Grade 7 and up) and −1 balloons that pull a side up (Grade 8) are tapped or dragged onto a side, and the beam tips to the heavier side. With Show number on, each side is written above (5 + 3 = 4 + 4; three or more equal parts in a row as 3 × 4 from Grade 3; with bags, 2x + 3 = 11) and a sign sits over the beam. Do: **Free**; **Make it balance** (dashed cubes show what the lighter side needs); **Solve** (Grade 4 and up: take 1, 10, 0.1 or a bag from both sides, add 1 or −1 to both and cancel 1 with −1 in Grade 8, split both sides into 2 to 5, peek in the bag, a new puzzle for the grade, and from Grade 6 a typed equation; every step is listed); **Try numbers** (Grade 4 and up: pick <, = or >, put a number in the bag, and each number tried goes on a number line, green when it works; with Show number the line shows every number that works). The Toolbox **Pattern builder** (`Manip.patterns`, on the same shelf, every grade) makes patterns four ways: **Repeat** (tap shapes to make the core, repeat it, show it as letters or claps, hide one, and from Grade 3 a table of the positions of each shape); **Numbers** (start at a number and add, take away, multiply or divide each time, on a number line, a number chart in Grades 1 to 3, a table and graph from Grade 4, the rule and any term from Grade 6, negative steps from Grade 7 and fraction steps in Grade 8); **Grow** (Grade 4 and up: tile figures that grow, or shrink from Grade 5, with the new tiles coloured, a table, a graph and from Grade 6 the rule and any step); **Compare** (Grade 7 and up: two patterns on one table and graph, and where they meet). The Toolbox **Data and chance** (`Manip.data`, on the Measure, money and data shelf, every grade) has four parts. **Sort**: 12 shapes (3 colours, 3 shapes, big and small) are tapped or dragged from the tray into groups by one rule (Grade 1), or by two rules in a Venn or Carroll diagram (Grade 2 and up); with Show number each part is counted, shapes in the wrong place get a dashed outline, and the readout says why the last shape goes where it goes. **Graph**: a survey with − and + for each answer, shown as a tally table, a picture graph (Grades 1 to 4; 1 picture = 2 in Grade 3), a line plot for number answers (Grade 2 and up), a bar graph (Grade 2 and up, with a scale from Grade 3 and fair share in Grade 3), two classes on a double-bar graph (Grade 4 and up), a stacked bar of parts of the whole (Grade 5 and up) and a circle graph (Grade 7 and up), with the mode, fractions (Grades 5 and 6) or percents (Grades 7 and 8) when shown. **Numbers** (Grade 4 and up): a list of numbers (tap one to take it off) as a stem-and-leaf plot, with the mean, median and mode worked out; Grade 6 adds a histogram and the range, Grade 7 says how the mean changed, and Grade 8 adds pairs on a scatter plot with a trend line and the relationship in words. **Chance**: a spinner or bag (set the parts), a coin or a die, tried 1, 10 or 100 times; grey bars show what should happen (Grade 4 and up) and coloured bars what did, and a probability line runs from impossible to certain (in words in Grades 1 to 3, fractions from Grade 4, percents from Grade 5). Grade 6 and up try two things at once (a table of every pair, and should against did for the last pair), and Grade 8 adds a tree diagram. The Toolbox **Grid and robot** (`Manip.grid`, on the Shapes and space shelf, every grade) has four parts. **Robot**: tap blocks to build code and Go runs it (Step goes one block at a time); the robot draws its path with the pen and stops when it bumps into a wall or the edge. Grades 1 and 2 use arrow blocks, and Grade 2 and up can run two robots at the same time. Grade 3 and up use forward, turn left, turn right, turn around and Repeat (a repeat inside a repeat from Grade 4). Grade 5 and up add If wall ahead (then and otherwise), Repeat until at the goal, and mazes; Grade 7 and up add My block (a small program used inside the code). With Show number the readout counts blocks, steps and turns, or says how far the robot is from the start in Grades 1 and 2. **Map** (Grades 1 to 3): put places on a grid map and tap two of them for directions (5 right, 2 up); Grade 1 adds position words (right of, above) and Grade 3 adds turns. **Plot** (Grade 4 and up): plot points from x and y or by tapping the grid, join them, and read how far apart they are; Grade 5 changes the scale (1 square = 2, 5 or 10) and Grade 6 uses four quadrants. **Move** (Grade 4 and up): slide or flip a shape, turn it from Grade 5, combine moves from Grade 6, and grow it (½, 2 or 3 times) from Grade 7; a table shows each corner before and after, and Grade 8 shows the rule, such as (x, y) → (y, −x). The Toolbox **3D solids and nets** (`Manip.solids`, on the Shapes and space shelf, every grade) draws solids in 3D that turn with a drag or the turn buttons. **Solids**: cube, prisms, pyramids, cylinder, cone and sphere (pentagonal and hexagonal ones from Grade 3); with Show number Grades 1 and 2 see the flat faces and whether it rolls, slides or stacks, and Grade 3 and up see the faces, edges and vertices. **Build**: tap the grid to stack cubes (or take them off); Grade 5 and up see the top, front and side views, and Grade 6 and up get views puzzles (build the solid from its three views). **Nets** (Grade 6 and up): a net folds and unfolds (the Fold slider or the Fold and Unfold buttons), each face shows its area, and the readout adds them into the surface area; a cylinder from Grade 7. **Volume** (Grade 7 and up): fill a rectangular prism, triangular prism or cylinder one layer at a time, with volume = base area × height, in cm³ and mL. Solids with flat faces are drawn by folding their nets, so a net and its solid always match. The Toolbox **Arrays and area model** (`Manip.areagrid` with `tool: true`, on the Parts and groups shelf, every grade; lessons keep the simpler area grid) has three parts. **Arrays**: rows and columns of dots (up to 12 by 12), seen as equal groups (rows of in Grade 1, groups of in Grade 2); from Grade 3 the readout gives the times and division facts, Turn it swaps rows and columns, and tapping between two columns splits the array (3 × 5 = 3 × 3 + 3 × 2). **Cover**: tap or drag over squares to cover a shape; from Grade 3 the area is in cm² with the perimeter, and from Grade 4 a rectangle reads length × width. **Area model** (Grade 4 and up): Multiply cuts a rectangle by place value and labels each part (34 × 6 = 180 + 24); two-digit by two-digit from Grade 5, a decimal tenth from Grade 6 (Tenths) and decimals by decimals from Grade 7. Divide builds the quotient in place-value chunks (384 ÷ 12 = 30 + 2) and shows what is left over, also as a fraction. The Toolbox **Shape kit** (`Manip.geoboard` with `tool: true`, on the Shapes and space shelf, every grade; lessons keep the simpler geoboard) has two parts. **Blocks**: pattern blocks (hexagon, trapezoid, blue rhombus, triangle, square, tan rhombus) added from a tray, dragged (a corner close to another block snaps to it), turned by 30°, flipped and taken off; Mirror shows each block reflected across a line. Show number counts the blocks and, from Grade 2, gives their area in green triangles. **Geoboard**: tap pegs to stretch a band (5 by 5 squares, 8 by 8 from Grade 3), tap the first peg to close it, and drag a corner. The readout names the shape and gives its sides and corners; from Grade 3 its perimeter and area; from Grade 4 its angle types, parallel sides and right-angle marks; from Grade 5 the triangle type and each angle in degrees; from Grade 6 the quadrilateral (rhombus, parallelogram, trapezoid, kite) and Diagonals; in Grade 8 Squares on sides for a right triangle (a² + b² = c²). The Toolbox **Measuring kit** (`Manip.scale` with `tool: true`, on the Measure, money and data shelf, every grade; lessons keep the simpler kitchen scale) has five parts. **Length**: a thing (pencil, crayon, ribbon, straw, leaf) measured with cubes or paper clips (Grades 1 to 3) or a ruler (Grade 2 and up, in cm; mm from Grade 4, also m from Grade 5); the thing can be dragged or moved off 0, and the readout then subtracts (14 − 2 = 12). **Mass**: a pan balance with a thing on one side and cubes (Grades 1 and 2) or 1 kg, 500 g, 100 g, 50 g and 10 g weights (Grade 3 and up) on the other; it tips until they balance; tap a weight to take it off. **Capacity**: fill a container and pour it into a measuring jug marked in cups (Grades 1 and 2) or mL (Grade 3 and up, 50 mL marks from Grade 4); the readout says how much it holds, in L as a decimal from Grade 5. **Angles** (Grade 4 and up): drag an arm or step it by 1° or 10°, with a right-angle card; a protractor to 180° with both scales from Grade 5, and a full-turn protractor with reflex angles from Grade 6. **Circles** (Grade 7 and up): drag the radius; Diameter, and Unroll lays the circle out as a little more than 3 diameters; the readout gives d, C and A. The Toolbox **Clock and calendar** (`Manip.clock` with `tool: true`, on the Measure, money and data shelf, Grades 1 to 4; lessons keep the simpler clock) has four parts. **Clock**: drag the minute hand (the hour hand follows) or the hour hand, or step the time (by half hours in Grade 1, by 1 or 5 minutes and hours from Grade 2); Show number adds the digital time and the time in words (half past, quarter to), with a.m. and p.m. from Grade 3; Run lets the second hand go. **Stopwatch** (Grade 2 and up): start and stop, in minutes and seconds. **Elapsed** (Grade 3 and up): a start and an end clock, and an open number line that jumps to the next hour, then by hours, then by minutes. **Calendar**: the month with Ontario holidays and the season; tap two days to count the days and weeks between. The Toolbox **Money** (`Manip.coins` with `tool: true`, on the Measure, money and data shelf, every grade; lessons keep the simpler coins and bills) has five parts. **Count**: tap Canadian coins and bills onto the mat (the $100 bill from Grade 2); Sort, Fewest coins and Break the biggest show the same amount other ways. **Shop** (Grade 3 and up): price tags (under a dollar and whole dollars in Grade 3, whole dollars in Grade 4, dollars and cents from Grade 5), a cart, money paid and the change, counted up from the cost; from Grade 5 Add HST (13%) and cash rounded to the nearest 5¢. **Budget** (Grade 5 and up): earn, spend, save and donate each week, with a saving goal week by week from Grade 6. **Interest** (Grade 6 and up): simple interest year by year, with compound interest beside it in Grade 8. **Exchange** (Grade 7 and up): Canadian dollars to US dollars, euros, pounds, pesos or yen and back, at sample rates. The Toolbox **Algebra tiles** (`Manip.tiles` with `tool: true`, on the Patterns and equations shelf, Grades 5 to 8; lessons keep the simpler tiles) has three parts. **Expressions**: x tiles and unit tiles (negative tiles from Grade 7); Group like terms simplifies (x + 1 + (−x) + x + (−1) = x), and Let x = evaluates. **Equations**: tiles on the left and right of =; Add to Left, Right or Both sides, tap a tile with Both sides on to take it from both, Remove zero pairs (Grade 7 and up), and Share the units among the x tiles; the answer is checked in the equation as first built. **Integers** (Grade 6 and up): +1 and −1 tiles, zero pairs, Take away (a zero pair is added when there is nothing to take), and the readout writes what was done (2 − (−2) = 4); in Grade 8 Make a × b puts groups on, or takes them away from zero pairs. The Toolbox **Place value disks** (`Manip.disks` with `tool: true`, on the Numbers shelf, Grades 3 to 7; lessons keep the simpler disks) has a column for each place: to thousands in Grade 3, ten thousands to hundredths in Grade 4, hundred thousands to thousandths in Grade 5 and millions to thousandths from Grade 6 (on a phone the columns scroll and the headings shorten to M, HTh, TTh and so on). Tap a disk in the tray to add it, a disk on the mat to take it off; Group 10 makes 1 of the next place, Break one turns the next disk tapped into 10 of the place to its right, and × 10 and ÷ 10 (Grade 5 and up) slide every disk one place. The readout gives the number and its expanded form, with powers of ten from Grade 7. The Toolbox **Times grid** (`Manip.times`, a new tool on the Parts and groups shelf, Grades 3 to 8) has four parts. **Times grid**: a 10 by 10 grid in Grade 3 and 12 by 12 from Grade 4; tap a product to light its rectangle, its turned fact and every other place with the same product. **Factors** (Grade 4 and up): every rectangle that a number of squares makes, the factors and factor pairs, and from Grade 6 prime or composite. **Primes** (Grade 6 and up): a 1 to 100 chart where the multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7 are crossed out in turn, leaving the 25 primes. **Squares** (Grade 7 and up): a number drawn as the biggest square it holds plus what is left, with its square root or the two whole numbers it lies between.

### Grade settings

Every grade runs the same app, so practice, the chapter quiz and the check-up work the same way in every grade. What differs is set in `gradeN/assets/grade.js` (the defaults are at the top of `assets/engine/site.js`):
- `grade`, `tools` (the Toolbox page) and `meta` (the chapter list: id, title, strand, expectation codes, description).
- `home`: the home page words (`hero`, `start`, `notes`), and for a grade with its own layout `intro`, `legend`, `contents` and `card` (Grade 1 numbers its chapter cards with small ten frames; Grade 8 uses its own card layout).
- Words and typing for younger children: `right` and `right2` (Grade 1 says "Yes, that is right."), and `input`: the keyboard (`numeric` in Grades 1 and 2), the box's placeholder, and `pad`, a big number pad under each answer box (Grade 1).
- `checkup`: the "How it works" list and the whole-grade check-ups. Grade 1 has a short one (10 questions, `#check-up/short`) and a longer one (`#check-up/all`); Grade 7 asks two questions from every chapter.
- Typed answers a grade reads its own way: `parseNum` (Grades 6 to 8 also accept a typed minus sign −; Grades 7 and 8 accept fractions and mixed numbers) and `judge` (Grade 7 can insist on lowest terms; Grade 8 compares very big and very small answers to 9 significant figures).
- Small drawing and number options: `svgScale` (Grade 1 draws pictures a little bigger), `svgLabelText`, `diskLabel` (Grade 4), `wholeDollars` (Grade 3 shows $5, not $5.00), `groupDecimals` (Grade 8 spaces long decimals: 0.333 333).
- `afterRoute`: runs after each page is drawn (Grade 7 draws pictures in as they scroll into view; Grade 8 fades sections in).

If a setting is needed by a second grade, use it there too; if a new behaviour is needed, add it to `assets/engine/site.js` (with a setting only when grades must differ), never to a grade file.

## A chapter

```
Atlas.register('numbers-1000', {
  lessons:  [ { id, title, concepts: [ { id, title, body, watch, try, key, mistake, check } ] } ],
  quiz:     [ fixed questions ],
  quizFresh: true,          // the chapter quiz is made fresh each time from the practice skills
  practice: [ { id, name, code: 'B1.1', gen: function (level) { return question; } } ]
});
```

- **Practice skills** make a new question every time, at level 1, 2 or 3. The site moves a child up a level after 3 right in a row, and down after 2 misses. Every fourth question reviews another skill of the chapter already started; "Mixed review" brings in skills from other chapters. A skill is **secure** after 3 right in a row at level 3 (in a full chapter: on two different days, see below).
- **Question shapes**: a skill can ask the same idea in several ways. `Atlas.Q.vary(skillId, level, [[minLevel, 'shape-name', fn], ...])` picks one, never the same shape twice in a row. See `QUESTIONS.md`.
- **Check-ups** use level 1 of every skill, then a few level-2 questions.
- **Idea cards**: each idea in a lesson shows as a small stack of cards, one at a time: "Watch" (or "Learn") with the explanation and clip, "Try it" with the key idea, then "Your turn". Back and Next move through the cards first, then to the other ideas. A part the idea doesn't have is left out. An idea with only one part shows as a single page.
- **Watch clips** (`Manip.player`) don't start on their own. The student taps Play, or steps one part at a time with ‹ and ›. The "Auto-play" switch on any clip turns auto-start on for every clip. It is saved in this browser as `atlas:autoplay`.
- **Full chapters**: a chapter with `full: true` adds a "Together" card (guided steps with prompts that fade), needs 4 right in "Your turn", shows a fix for known wrong answers, and brings ideas back in a warm-up on a schedule. See "Full chapters" in `QUESTIONS.md`.
- **Worked examples ("My turn")**: an idea can add `example` (the same shape as `watch`). It gets its own "My turn" card after the first card: one example solved step by step in "I" language ("My turn. First I..."), played like a Watch clip. It has no voice: Noel tried the device's built-in voice and found it too robotic.

## Printable worksheets

Grown-ups (grown-up view only) get a **Worksheets** tab on a chapter that has worksheets. Each idea has five sheets, from most help to least: **Learn** (Watch me, Together, On your own), **Practise 1, 2 and 3**, and **Think** (reasoning and explaining). Each sheet can be printed in 10 themes, with or without its answer key as page 2. Grade 3 has worksheets for Numbers to 1000 (17 ideas), Adding and Subtracting (16 ideas), Patterns, Equations and Code (17 ideas) and Multiplying and Dividing (19 ideas): 345 sheets, 3450 with the themes. Grade 4 has worksheets for Numbers to 10 000, Adding and Subtracting, Multiplying and Dividing, Fractions, Decimals, Patterns and Algebra, Data, Shapes, Angles and Grids, Measuring, and Money: every chapter (96 ideas, 480 sheets).
- `assets/engine/sheets.js` draws one US Letter page as a picture (SVG, 612 by 792 points) from a sheet's questions, and runs the Worksheets tab: `#chapter/sheets` (the list, the theme buttons and the answer key switch), `#chapter/sheets/all` (every sheet at once) and `#chapter/sheets/<idea>/<kind>` (one sheet, its key and a Print button; the browser's print gives exactly one page per sheet). The chosen theme and key switch are saved in this browser (`atlas:sheet-theme`, `atlas:sheet-key`).
- Every sheet uses one **Editorial** layout (Noel chose it on 2026-11-08): the chapter and kind in small spaced capitals, a large title with the idea's goal ("I can ...") under it, the Watch me or Example with the idea's tip beside it, questions in two columns with their numbers hanging in the margin and hairlines between rows, answers on short lines (no boxes), and a "How did it feel?" check-in at the bottom. The key puts what to watch for, the Ontario codes and a score line there instead.
- `assets/engine/sheet-themes.js` holds the 10 themes. A theme sets its words (two names, the things to count and what holds them, so stories read "Ava puts 5 pucks in each of 4 bags"), its own font (loaded in `sheets.css`) for the title, numbers and the example's sum, one accent colour, a small line-art mark, a light ornament at the page edge, and the things and holders in the pictures. Noel's rule: the paper is always white and colour is light (thin outlines in one colour, no filled bands). The numbers are the same in every theme, so no theme is harder. Practise 3 and Think draw plain dots instead of the theme's things.
- `assets/engine/sheet-pics.js` adds the pictures most chapters need (base ten blocks, place value charts, ten frames, column sums, fractions, patterns, clocks, coins and price tags, bar graphs and tallies, 3D objects, area grids, rectangles, rulers, balances, number lines, maps, Venn diagrams). Its top lists each picture's options. Pictures for Grades 4 to 8 go in `sheet-pics-upper.js`. A picture file adds to `Atlas.sheets.pics` using the drawing helpers in `Atlas.sheets.draw`. A picture with its own lines to write on takes the question's first answers. Every picture needs an example in `tests/verify/sheets.js` (`__picSamples`), and, if it shows a number or has lines to write on, a reader there (`READ`).
- `gradeN/sheets/<chapter>.js` holds the questions, written by hand (not made at random), so a sheet always prints the same. The top of `grade3/sheets/multiply-divide.js` lists the helpers and the question fields (`eq` sums with `[]` boxes and `a` answers in order, `pic` pictures, `pick` choices to circle, `draw` and `lines` for open answers, each with an example on the key).
- `assets/engine/sheet-pics-upper.js` adds the pictures Grades 4 to 8 need: an area model split by place value (`amodel`), the standard written method for × (`mult`), a place value chart for × and ÷ by 10, 100 and 1000 (`pvmove`), place value disks, a rounding hill, a hundred grid or tenths strips, sharing with a pile left over (`shareR`), division in chunks, fraction bars (`fbars`), number lines in tenths or fractions (`dline`) circles and squares for a ratio (`mix`), a growing pattern of tiles (`grow`), a short program (`code`), bar graphs that can be double, stacked or start above 0 (`dbar`), a line graph (`lineg`), a spinner (`spin`), a triangle with its sides or angles (`tri`), a cube building seen from above with each stack's height (`heights`) a coordinate grid with points, a shape, a slide or a flip line (`coord`), an angle on its own or on a protractor (`angle`) and a rectangle, parallelogram or triangle with its base and dashed height (`plane`). A blank in a sum may sit inside brackets: `2 × (5 + []) = []`. Its top lists each picture's options.
- In a sum, numbers of 5 or more digits get a space (12 500; the sheet keeps it on one line), and decimals, fractions and dollar amounts (3.75, 3/4, $4.50) are drawn as numbers. Each line to write on is long enough for its answer. Choices that do not fit on one line go on to the next. `t ÷ d = [] R []` is a division with a remainder, "23 hundreds" can be used in a sum, `[]/8` and `3/[]` put the line in one part of a fraction, `2 3/4` is a mixed number, and `3 : 5` is a ratio.
- Every sheet has a number such as G3-MD-07-P2-RK (grade, chapter, idea, kind, theme). Each idea in `gradeN/sheets/<chapter>.js` has a `goal` (the "I can" line) and a `watch` note for the key; its lesson's `cue` is the tip.
- The checks draw every sheet in every theme and work out every answer again (`tests/verify/sheets.js`); see `CHECKS.md`.

## Math Plan

`plan/index.html` loads `vis.js` (pictures), `bank.js` (placement questions), `ladder.js` (every Ontario expectation, Grades 1 to 8, in the Ministry's wording), `blurbs.js` (one short plain-language line for each expectation), `families.js` (concept families), `engine.js` (placement and plan logic; the placement check asks at each grade until the answers clearly show secure or not yet, a sequential probability ratio test set by `E.P_SECURE`, `E.P_NOT` and `E.PLACE_ERR`, and `tests/run.js` replays it with pretend students) and `app.js` (the screens). The plain line is what children and parents see, for example on "Today's idea" and when a concept-map square is tapped. The Ministry wording is shown under "Ontario wording". When an expectation is added to `ladder.js`, add its line to `blurbs.js` too, or the checks fail.

`families.js` sorts every expectation into one **concept family**: one idea that grows across the grades, such as Fractions, Time or Budgets. Each expectation is in exactly one family, or the checks fail. The concept map uses the families for its "By idea" view (one row per family, Grades 1 to 8 across); tapping a family's name lists that idea in every grade with a link to each lesson. The same list is the set of sibling lessons to check when one lesson in the family is improved (see CLAUDE.md). When an expectation is added to `ladder.js`, add it to its family too.

## Saved progress

Progress is kept in the browser (`localStorage`, prefix `gNatlas:`, for example `g3atlas:sk:<skill id>` for a practice skill, `best:<chapter>` for the best quiz score, `checkups`, `cov`, `plan`, and `cn:<idea>` for lessons). The shared app saves exactly the keys and shapes each grade saved before it was shared; the checks load an older saved state in every grade to make sure it still shows. When a child is signed in, `assets/sync.js` copies it to Firebase so parents and tutors see it.

## Review notes

A way for Noel (or any parent or tutor) to leave tweaks while looking through the lessons, instead of one message at a time.
- `assets/sync.js` loads `assets/review.js` on every page that loads sync.js, for a signed-in parent or tutor, or when nobody is signed in (the note then asks them to sign in). Never for a student.
- The **Note** button shows only in grown-up view (`html.adult`). It opens a small sheet that says which screen the note is about: grade, lesson, idea, card (for example "Your turn card") and the question in view, plus the page address.
- Notes are saved to `users/{uid}/reviews/{id}` with `text`, `where`, `q`, `url`, `site`, `at`, `done`. `firestore.rules` lets only that parent or tutor read, change or delete them; students cannot write any.
- `family.js` shows **My notes** on the parent and tutor page (`family.html#notes`): To do and Done lists, Open screen, Mark done, Delete, and **Copy notes to send**, which copies the to-do list as plain text to paste to Claude.
- Language pages get the same button once they load `assets/sync.js` (the same file, from this repository).

## One app, two repositories

Atlas is one website at https://ontario-math-atlas.web.app with one sign-in, but its code lives in two repositories so a change to one subject cannot break the other:

- **math-atlas** (this one): the home page, Curriculum page, sign-in, family pages, Math Plan and the Math grades.
- **language-atlas**: the Language grades, with their own engine, checks and changelog. Published under `/language/`.

How publishing works:
1. A merge to `main` in either repository runs that repository's checks.
2. language-atlas then sends a `language-updated` event to this repository. Its publish workflow (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`) runs on that event too.
3. The publish workflow copies language-atlas `main` into `language/` and publishes the whole site once. Both repositories need the `ATLAS_TOKEN` secret (`SETUP.md`).

What they share at runtime (same website, so same browser storage and sign-in):
- Language pages load `assets/sync.js`, `fb.js` and `family.css` from here, so their progress saves to the family account as `students/{id}/progress/l1` … `l8`. `SITES` in `assets/fb.js` lists every Math (`g1`…) and Language (`l1`…) grade, and the family page and home page show both.
- The Language placement check (`language/placement/`) saves to `students/{id}/progress/lplace` (its whole state as `data.state`). `assets/langplace.js` reads it: one line on each child's card on the home page, and a **Language placement check** section on the Progress and notes page (secure grade and starting lesson for each of the 7 parts, reading aloud, writing sample). `lplace` is not in `SITES`, because it is not a grade.
- Both placement checks are linked from the same places. A child takes them on their own sign-in; a grown-up sees the results. Home page: the Math and Language panels each go from "Start the placement check" to "Continue the check" to the plan or grade (`home.js`). Account menu, the student page (`family.js`) and the top of each Curriculum list: Math Plan (`plan/`) and Language placement (`language/placement/`). Grown-ups: each child's card and Progress and notes have **Math Plan** (`plan/?sid=`) and **Language placement** (`family.html#s/{id}/lplace`, which opens Progress and notes at the Language placement section; `placeLink()` in `langplace.js`).
- The Curriculum page reads `language/index.html` to see which Language grades are ready, then each `language/gradeN/grade.js` for its chapters. Keep `Atlas.setup({ grade, chapters: [{ id, title, sub, strand }] })` in that shape.
- Browser storage: Math grades use `gNatlas:` keys, Language grades `lang-gN:`. Both use `atlas:adult` for the grown-up view.
