# Automatic checks

The checks in `tests/run.js` stand in for a careful human tester. They run:
- on every pull request (workflow **Checks**);
- before every publish (workflow **Publish to Firebase**). If any check fails, nothing is published and the live site stays as it was.

## What they check

1. **Repository rules.** Every script parses. The shared engine (the grade app `site.js`, lessons, toolbox and answer types) exists only once, in `assets/engine/`, and every grade page loads it, with its own `assets/grade.js` settings. Every Math Plan expectation in `plan/ladder.js` has one short plain-language line in `plan/blurbs.js` (140 characters at most).
2. **Every question.** Each practice skill is made 40 times at each level. Every lesson "Your turn" check and every written quiz question is checked too:
   - the question fits its type (for example, the right answer is one of the options);
   - nothing on screen says "undefined" or "NaN";
   - every newer question shape has its answer worked out again by `tests/verify/`.
3. **Every page.** Every chapter, lesson screen, practice page, quiz and Toolbox tool opens with no errors. On a phone-size screen the Toolbox blocks are tapped and dragged like a child would: add ones, drag a one into Tens to snap 10 ones into a ten, drag a ten off the mat, and Undo. The Toolbox number line is used the same way: put a marker on the line, jump by 10 twice, tap the line between 30 and 40 to zoom in, zoom out, and Undo. The Toolbox counters too: tap 12 counters onto two ten-frames ("10 and 2"), turn one over (11 + 1), share them into 5 groups (2 in each, 2 left over), drag one off the mat, and Undo. No number may show before Show number is on. The Toolbox number chart: count by 5 (20 squares coloured), jump from 23 to 47 ("23 + 24 = 47"), and Undo. The Toolbox fraction kit: tap three fourths ("3/4", or "3 fourths" in Grades 1 and 2), split them into eighths (6/8), Undo, pack 7 fourths into 1 3/4, share 7 by 2 (3 1/2 each), put one share on the mat, and drag a third onto the mat. In Grades 4 and up it also: makes 0.37 from decimal pieces (0.3 in Grade 4) and checks the grid (30 tenth squares) and the Symbols window (37/100 and 37%), makes 5 groups of 1/4 and Undoes one, and counts halves to 10. From Grade 5 it joins 6/8 into 3/4, combines 3/8 and 2/8, and fits fourths into 3 (12). From Grade 6 it adds 1/2 and 1/3 with same-size pieces (5/6) and lines up a typed 65% and 3/4. In Grades 7 and 8 it works out 3/4 × 2/3 (6/12 = 1/2, with 6 small parts in the Area window), fits 2/3 into 3/4 (1 1/8, also in twelfths), finds 5/12 between 1/3 and 1/2, and lines up −3/4 and −1/2. Grade 8 also does 1 1/2 × 2/3 = 1 and types 150% (1 1/2) and 0.5% (1/200). From Grade 3 it uses Ratio: 3 groups of 1 red and 5 blue (1 to 5 = 3 to 15, in Grade 3), 3 apples for each of 4 children (12, in Grade 4), 2 : 3 four times as much (8 : 12) from Grade 5, 3 : 2 juice as 60% of the mix and 4 apples for $6 as $1.50 for 1 from Grade 6, adding 2 to both of 2 : 3 (4 : 5, off the graph's line) from Grade 7, and in Grade 8 compares 3 : 2 with 3 : 5 (A is stronger) and solves 3 : 5 = ? : 20 (12). The Toolbox balance: 5 + 3 balances 4 + 4, Make it balance shows 1 dashed cube after one is taken off, and from Grade 3 3 × 4 balances 6 + 6. From Grade 4 it tries 4 in x + 3 < 9 (true for x < 6), and Grades 4 to 6 solve a new puzzle by taking the same from both sides. Grade 6 solves a typed 2x + 3 = 11, Grade 7 3x + 2 = x + 8 and Grade 8 x + 4 = −2 with balloons. The Toolbox pattern builder: a core of 3 shapes repeated 3 times reads A B B, from Grade 3 position 7 of A B C is a red circle, numbers start at 2 and add 2, Grade 4 and up grow tiles with a table and a graph, Grade 6 and up show the rule 3n + 1 and step 20 (61), Grade 7 and up see 2n + 5 meet 4n + 1 at step 2, and Grade 8 starts at 6 and adds −2. The Toolbox data and chance tool: Sort them all puts 4 red and 8 not red in Grade 1, and from Grade 2 2 shapes in the middle of a red and triangle Venn diagram and 6 outside, and a big red triangle tapped into the middle reads "red and a triangle"; the first survey reads apple (7) of 18, soccer (12) of 36 or bus 42% of 200 by grade, and one more tally adds 1; from Grade 4 118 to 140 cm has mean 128, median 127.5 and mode 124 (range 22 from Grade 6, and taking off 140 moves the mean to 126.7 in Grade 7), and Grade 8's height and arm span pairs read strong, positive; a spinner with 2 red parts of 4 gives red 1/2 (as likely as not in Grades 1 to 3) and 10 spins read 10 spins; from Grade 6 a coin and a die have 12 outcomes, each 1/12, and the table counts all 10 tries; Grade 8 draws the tree for two coins (HH 1/4). The Toolbox grid and robot: in Grades 1 and 2 the arrows right, right, up, up, right put the robot 3 right and 2 up from the start, and from Grade 3 repeat 4 times (forward, forward, turn right) is 8 steps and 4 turns, back at the start; in Grades 1 to 3 the map reads 5 right, 2 up from the school to the swings; from Grade 4 plotting (1, 4) then (5, 2) reads 4 right, 2 down and the first slide (3 right, 1 down) takes corner A 3 right and 1 down, and Grade 8 turns a shape a quarter turn about (0, 0) with the rule (x, y) → (y, −x). The Toolbox 3D solids and nets: a cylinder has 2 flat faces and can roll, slide and stack in Grades 1 and 2, and from Grade 3 a triangular prism has 5 faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices; three taps stack 3 cubes and Undo takes one off; from Grade 5 the three views are drawn; from Grade 6 the 4 × 3 × 2 box net has a surface area of 52 cm² and Fold closes it; from Grade 7 a 4 × 3 × 3 box filled layer by layer is 36 cm³ = 36 mL and a cylinder of radius 2 and height 3 is about 37.7 cm³. The Toolbox arrays and area model: 3 rows of 4 reads as 3 rows of 4 (Grade 1), 3 groups of 4 (Grade 2) or 12 ÷ 3 = 4 (Grade 3 and up), and from Grade 3 a 3 by 5 array split after column 3 reads 3 × 3 + 3 × 2 = 15; A rectangle covers 12 squares (12 cm² with a perimeter of 14 cm from Grade 3, 4 × 3 = 12 from Grade 4); from Grade 4 the area model shows 34 × 6 = 180 + 24 (Grade 4) or 23 × 14 = 200 + 80 + 30 + 12 (Grade 5 and up), and divides 96 ÷ 4 or 384 ÷ 12. 4. **Answering like a child.** On a phone-size screen (390 px wide), practice questions of every kind are answered right, and sometimes wrong first. They must be marked correctly. Nothing may run off the side of the screen. Fresh quizzes answered all right must score 7 of 7.
5. **Saved progress.** In every grade, progress saved the way the grade has always saved it (skill levels, secure skills, a quiz score, a check-up, the plan) still shows on the home and check-up pages, and practice saves back under the same keys in the same shape.
6. **Worksheets.** Every printable worksheet is drawn in every theme, with and without its answer key, in the real fonts (this one check may fetch Google Fonts). Nothing may run past the bottom of the page, outside the page or out of its question's column, and no word may be left unfilled. Every answer on every key is worked out again from the question by `tests/verify/sheets.js`: the sums with the answers filled in must be true, a picture must match its sum, a story's answer must follow from its numbers (decimals may differ only by rounding), a remainder must be less than the divisor, a story that rounds a remainder up or down must do so correctly, numbers written in words, rounded or put in order must be right, a fraction asked for in simplest form must be in simplest form, a fraction read from a picture must use the picture's own parts (3/4, not 6/8), a ratio must use the picture's or the story's amounts in the order asked, equivalent ratios must name the same comparison, every row of a ratio table must keep the same rate, a pattern's next terms must keep its step or repeat its core, a figure's tiles must follow the pattern's rule, and a value for a letter must make its expression, equation or inequality come out right, a mean, median, mode or range must come from the data, a relative frequency from its count and the total, a probability from the spinner's equal parts, and the number of times a bar looks taller from where its scale starts, a triangle's kind must come from its sides or angles, a missing angle must make 180°, three sides make a triangle only if the two short ones add to more than the long one, a cube building's front and side views must be its tallest stacks, a point read from a grid must be the one drawn, a point after a slide or flip must land where the move puts it, amounts in different metric units must be equal once put in one unit (3.5 m = 350 cm), a unit or an estimate must fit the thing's real size, an angle's kind, its closest benchmark and a protractor reading must come from the angle drawn, an area must use the base and the height (not the slanted side), halved for a triangle, and a perimeter must go all the way around, each part of an area model and each line of a written method must be right, and each circled choice must be the only right one (or, for an estimate, the closest). Every worksheet picture is also drawn once from its example and must stay inside a question's column. The Worksheets tab must open on a phone without running off the side, and printing a sheet with its key must give exactly 2 pages.

## Running them yourself

```
cd tests
npm install                 # first time only
npx playwright install chromium   # first time only
npm test                    # everything (several minutes)
npm run quick               # fewer tries, faster
node run.js --grade 3       # one grade
```

The last line says **ALL CHECKS PASSED** or lists each problem with the grade, chapter and skill it came from.
