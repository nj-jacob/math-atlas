# Changelog

Newest first. One line per change, in plain English.

## 2026-11-04
- Toolbox **Number chart** (was Hundred chart), rebuilt: choose 1 to 100, 101 to 200 or 0 to 99. Tap squares to colour them with one of 3 colours. **Skip count by** 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 or 25 colours the pattern one square after another; two patterns can share a square (a ring shows the second colour), so where counting by 2 and by 5 meet (10, 20, 30) stands out. **Jump from… to…**: tap two numbers to see the jump as tens down and ones across (23 to 47 is +10 +10 and +1 +1 +1 +1, so 23 + 24 = 47). **Hide some** blanks a few numbers to work out; tap one to see it. Numbers in the readout show only with Show number.
- Checks: the drag test now waits for a tool that redraws itself (for example when its number appears), so a slow computer can't make the check stop early. This had blocked publishing the counters.
- Toolbox **Counters and ten-frames**, one tool in place of the old separate Counters and Ten-frames: tap or drag red and yellow counters from the tray onto the mat. Choose a free mat, 1 ten-frame or 2 ten-frames (a counter dropped on a frame snaps into a box). Tap a counter to turn it over (red to yellow). Drag one off the mat to put it back. **Pair up** shows even and odd, **Rows of ten** shows tens and ones, and **Share into** splits the counters into 2 to 10 equal groups with any left over. With Show number on: "8 + 6 = 14, that is 10 and 4", "7 pairs, none left over", "4 in each, 2 left over". Up to 100 counters. A new check uses it like a child would.

## 2026-11-03
- Toolbox **Number line**, rebuilt for Grades 1 to 3 (every grade gets it until the older-grade version): choose 0 to 20, 0 to 100, 0 to 1 000 or a blank line. Drag up to 3 coloured markers onto the line to compare numbers. Make jumps by dragging the round handle by a marker, or with the Jump by buttons (forward or back by 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50 or 100). Tap the line between two numbers to zoom in (30 to 40 shows every one). Nearest ten and Nearest hundred shade the tens or hundreds around a marker and mark halfway, for rounding. Numbers and the jump sum show only with Show number. On a phone the line stands up so the numbers have room.
- Grade 3 multiplying and dividing: a few true-or-false questions could rarely show a "wrong" claim that was actually right (for example "2 × 2 = 4" marked false). Fixed.
- Toolbox, first step of the new hands-on tool box: the tool now fills the screen under a slim bar (back, Undo, Show number, Tools). The **Tools** menu groups each grade's tools on five shelves (Numbers; Parts and groups; Patterns and equations; Shapes and space; Measure, money and data), each with a small picture. **Place value blocks** (pilot) are now dragged from a tray onto the mat: drag a block off the mat to put it back, drag a ten into Ones to break it, drag a one into Tens to snap 10 ones into a ten (or tap the buttons that appear). Extra hundreds pile up neatly instead of making the page longer. A "Tap to count" switch picks what a tap does: **Whole blocks** lights a block and shows its value (100, 10 or 1); **Small squares** lights a square inside a hundred or a ten and numbers it 1, 2, 3 and so on. The number stays hidden until **Show number** is switched on, so the child does the counting. Base-ten blocks everywhere, lessons included, now look like solid blocks. A new check drags the blocks like a child would. Place value blocks now have a fourth column, **Thousands**, with a thousand cube (snap 10 hundreds into a thousand, or break one). The mat stays one size on each device: as more blocks go on, the blocks get smaller to fit instead of the mat getting longer. In a pile of hundreds or thousands, the blocks at the back can be tapped too, and "Drag blocks here" sits in the middle of the mat. With **Show number** on, the number and the place value chart now sit above the mat, in plain view (the mat gets a little shorter to make room). The thousand cube now shows 10 rows on its top and side, and with **Small squares** a tap on it lights one layer and counts 100, 200, 300 and so on.

A skill in a full chapter is secure only after strong work on two different days, in every grade. Saved progress is kept, and a new check makes sure it still loads.

New answer checks catch the pyramid and cash-change errors.
- Grade 3 Fractions rebuilt as the first **full chapter** (pilot): 19 ideas in 6 lessons, each with its own practice skill. Every idea has a new **Together** card (the child does each step, with prompts that fade over 3 problems), 5 "Your turn" questions about that idea (4 right marks "Got it"), and fixes that name the exact mistake after a wrong answer. Ideas come back in a short **warm-up** 1, 3, 7 and 21 days later. A skill is secure only after strong work on two different days. New automatic checks stop guessable questions and levels that don't get harder in full chapters.
- Parent pages: Language placement check results. Each child's card on the home page has a line for it, and Progress and notes shows the secure grade and starting lesson in each of the 7 parts, the reading-aloud result and the writing mark. Signing out now also clears the placement check from the device.
- Math Plan concept map: a new "By idea" view. Every expectation now belongs to one of 41 idea families (for example Fractions, Rounding, Time, Budgets), shown one row per family with Grades 1 to 8 across. Tapping a family's name lists that idea in every grade, with a link to each lesson. Tapping a square also links to its family. The checks fail if an expectation is in no family or in two. A new rule in CLAUDE.md: when a lesson is improved, check the same idea in the other grades.
- Pages always load fresh: web pages (like the Language list of ready grades) are no longer kept in the browser for up to an hour, so a newly published grade no longer shows "Coming soon".
- Review notes for grown-ups: in grown-up view, every grade page has a **Note** button. A parent or tutor types a tweak and the note saves to their account with the exact screen and question. **My notes** on the parent page lists them (to do and done), with a button to copy them all to send to Claude. Students never see the button or the notes.
